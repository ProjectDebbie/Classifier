Fabrication of a three dimensional spongy scaffold using human Wharton's jelly
derived extra cellular matrix for wound healing.
P The Wharton's jelly  WJ  contains significant amounts of extracellular matrix
ECM  components and rich source of endogenous growth factors. In this study, we
designed a new biomimetic spongy scaffold from decellularized WJ derived ECM and
used it as a skin substitute. Histological analysis and biochemical assays showed
that bio active molecules preserved in the fabricated scaffolds and that the
scaffolds have highly interconnected porous structure. Cytotoxicity and
mechanical evaluation of the scaffold indicated that it is non toxic and has
appropriate mechanical properties. MTT assay, SEM and histological analysis of
human fibroblast, seeded on the scaffolds, confirmed cellular viability,
attachment, penetration and proliferation. The effectiveness of WJ derived
scaffolds in the regeneration of full thickness wound was assessed through an in
vivo experiment. Our results demonstrated that the scaffolds were well integrated
into the mouse tissue and absorbed the exudates after one week. Unlike the
controls, in WJ group there were not only complete wound closing and
disappearance of the scab, but also complete reepithelialization, newly generated
epidermal layers and appendages after 12days of implantation. Taken together, our
results indicate that WJ derived scaffolds are able to improve attachment,
penetration and growth of the fibroblast cells and speed up the healing
processes, which would offer a proper skin graft for wound healing.
